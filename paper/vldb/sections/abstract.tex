% 论文摘要：围绕研究问题、共形几何障碍、极坐标翘曲乘积解耦思想与已验证实测证据展开
Hyperbolic embeddings in the {Poincar\'e} ball and {Lorentz} hyperboloid represent hierarchical, taxonomic, and entailment relations with substantially lower distortion than flat {Euclidean} spaces, supporting retrieval-augmented generation, vision-language alignment, and knowledge-base search.
Serving million-scale approximate nearest neighbor ({ANN}) queries over curved manifolds, however, exposes a fundamental tension between geometric fidelity and memory efficiency: flat inner-product indices distort geodesic order across radial shells, whereas native hyperbolic graph indices either fetch full-precision coordinates at every traversal hop or quantize ambient coordinates without decoupling conformal scales.
Transplanting flat proximity-graph pruning and vector compression to hyperbolic space also fails.
Because negative curvature bends geodesics toward the origin and scales directional inner products by the singular conformal factor $(1 - c\|x\|_2^2)^{-1}$, interior hubs geodesically occlude entire spherical neighborhoods during {Vamana} pruning, shared-query quantization errors fail to cancel across radial shells, and tight boundary clusters suffer margin-to-noise ratio ({$\MNR$}) collapse.

We present \textbf{\rquiver{}}, a warped-product proximity-graph index for hyperbolic vector search.
\rquiver{} decouples exact single-precision radial and conformal scalars in a 32-byte vertex header from scale-invariant spherical directional codes---instantiated with training-free 2-bit Sign-Magnitude ({SM}) bitplanes---deferring full-vector double-precision geodesic evaluation to a compact reranking tail.
Analyzing how hyperbolic conformal weights and geodesic curvature interact with polar directional quantization, we establish query boundary-depth invariance on isobathic shells, characterize cross-shell query-noise non-cancellation, derive a deterministic parameter-free orthogonal-projection estimator, and introduce a purity-gated local tangent-cone residual code that attenuates tangent-direction error with zero per-vertex byte overhead and without global random rotations.
Guided by these geometric properties, we construct a warped-product fiber-bundle graph inside-out using {QuadriPrune}---partitioning each vertex's out-degree across four geometric channels (stem-excess geodesic fibers, radially shielded isobathic rings restoring same-shell error cancellation, and inward/outward radial elevators)---paired with degree-preserving spanning-tree chord swaps that achieve full entry reachability on the measured graphs.
An execution engine co-locates polar headers, variance-stabilized 256-bit early beam sketches, and 2-bit bitplanes within single $4096$\,B OS pages to avoid cross-array secondary {DTLB} walks per accessed record and evaluates bitplane inner products via a 3-to-2 carry-save {AVX2} kernel.

Across eight real and synthetic hyperbolic benchmarks on {AMD EPYC 9654} ({Zen~4}), \rquiver{} builds indices $5.44\times$ faster ($35.0$\,s vs.\ $190.2$\,s on 32 threads on {Cohere-1M}) than full-precision polar graphs and shrinks logical hot navigation memory by $2.60\times$ ($2.44\times$ page-co-located) relative to 8-bit polar quantization.
At $99\%$ Recall@10 on {Cohere-1M}, \rquiver{} improves throughput over full-precision polar traversal by $4.30\times$ on 1 thread ($1{,}930$ vs.\ $448$\,QPS) and $6.31\times$ on 24 threads ($42{,}466$ vs.\ $6{,}735$\,QPS); on this benchmark, {HyperspaceDB} ($3.23$\,GB) peaks at $96.78\%$ Recall@10 ($290$ vs.\ $4{,}939$\,QPS on 1 thread at $95\%$ recall) and centered {Faiss} peaks at $98.91\%$ ($127$ vs.\ $2{,}907$\,QPS on 1 thread at $98\%$ recall).
On native hyperbolic {MERU-1M}, \rquiver{} delivers $53{,}483$\,QPS ($99.25\%$ Recall@10, $3.37\times$--$3.88\times$ over {HyperspaceDB} FP32), reaches $65{,}405$\,QPS ($99.36\%$) on {WordNet-Hazy}, and raises synthetic {Clustered-Narrow} recall from $34.22\%$ to $99.385\%$ at $L=256$ ($99.80\%$ at $L=384$) ($1.84\times$--$4.56\times$ higher $99\%$-recall QPS than {HyperspaceDB} FP32), while characterizing a configuration-dependent recall trade-off on isotropic narrow shells.

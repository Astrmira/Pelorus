\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\rquiver{} uses radial depth and angular direction at different stages of hyperbolic search. On shallow native {MERU-1M}, QuadriPrune improves recall at a fixed beam budget, while starting at the origin hubs alone gives higher throughput at nearly identical recall. Residual coding matters in a different regime. On deep {HyperE WordNet}, it halves the beam needed to exceed $90\%$ recall, though both variants require a beam of $16{,}384$ to exceed $95\%$ in our sweep.

Separating navigation from reranking also creates two independent storage decisions. Page packing saves $58.1$\,MiB of hot pages on {MERU-1M}. Moving its cold FP32 vectors to SSD reduces process RSS at the cost of throughput and tail latency. Both FP16 cold formats retain over $99.9\%$ recall on this shallow dataset. At extreme WordNet depths even FP32 coordinate storage changes nearest-neighbor identities, so encoding precision must be evaluated against the source coordinates.

The index serves static snapshots. Construction uses a global depth order, sampled routing and dictionary statistics, and a final directed reachability pass. Continuous updates would require maintaining these structures as the depth and direction distribution changes. The largest native workload here is shallow {MERU-1M}. Measurements on deep native embeddings cover the smaller {HyperE WordNet} corpus.

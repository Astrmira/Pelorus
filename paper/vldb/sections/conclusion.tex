\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{\rquiver{}}, a warped-product proximity-graph index for hyperbolic vector search in the {Poincar\'e} ball and {Lorentz} hyperboloid.
By factoring the warped-product manifold into unquantized FP32 radial and conformal scalars paired with scale-invariant 2-bit Sign-Magnitude spherical codes, \rquiver{} prevents quantization noise from entering singular boundary denominators while compressing logical hot navigation state to $352$\,B per vertex ($485.0$\,MB split or $517.0$\,MB page-co-located at $N=10^6$).
Our conformal polar distortion and hyperbolic graph-pathology analysis characterized query boundary-depth invariance, cross-shell query-noise non-cancellation, radial occlusion and angular-cone collapse, and boundary cluster {$\MNR$} collapse, directly motivating stem-excess {QuadriPrune} construction, spanning-tree chord-swap island repair, routing via the constrained radial minimizer, deterministic orthogonal-projection de-biasing, purity-gated zero-byte-per-node tangent-cone residuals, and $4096$\,B single-page co-location.
Across eight real and synthetic hyperbolic benchmarks on {AMD Zen~4}, \rquiver{} accelerates 32-thread index construction by $5.44\times$, reduces logical hot navigation memory by $2.60\times$ ($2.44\times$ page-co-located) relative to 8-bit polar quantization, improves $99\%$-recall throughput by $4.30\times$ (1T) to $6.31\times$ (24T) over full-precision polar search on {Cohere-1M}, reaches $99.70\%$ peak Recall@10 on {Cohere-1M} (vs.\ $96.78\%$ for {HyperspaceDB} and $98.91\%$ for centered {Faiss}) alongside $53{,}483$\,QPS at $99\%$ recall on native hyperbolic {MERU-1M} ($3.37\times$--$3.88\times$ over {HyperspaceDB} FP32), and raises synthetic {Clustered-Narrow} recall from $34.22\%$ to $99.385\%$ at $L=256$ ($99.80\%$ at $L=384$).
The synthetic stress tests further delineate how manifold geometry shapes these gains: at $\ge 99\%$ recall, \rquiver{} exceeds {HyperspaceDB} FP32 flat throughput by $1.84\times$ / $2.92\times$ (1T / 24T) on {Clustered-Narrow} and $3.68\times$ / $4.56\times$ on {Clustered-Wide}, whereas on isotropic {Uniform-Narrow} \rquiver{} ($M/L_c = 16/128$) peaks at $64.735\%$ Recall@10 compared with $92.17\%$ for {HyperspaceDB} ($32/256$), measuring a topology--budget trade-off on unclustered narrow shells. All four real datasets activate zero residual cones and use global directional codes.
